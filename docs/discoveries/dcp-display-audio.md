# Discoveries: DCP display audio on the M1 Max

Machine: MacBook Pro M1 Max, macOS 27.2 (26B123), lid closed, three Dell monitors on USB-C.

## 2026-11-03

- All three monitors arrive via USB-C DP alt mode (`atc0/1/2-dpphy`), none via the built-in HDMI port.
  | Monitor | DCP | CoreAudio UID | CG vendor/model | Notes |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | U2518D, speakers | `dcpext0` | `10AC3C41-0000-0000-041D-010380371F78` | `0x10AC` / `0x413C` | Via hub's DP-to-HDMI converter, transport `hdmi`, main display |
  | U2520D | `dcpext1` | `10AC50A1-0000-0000-231E-0104A5371F78` | `0x10AC` / `0xA150` | DisplayPort |
  | U2518D | `dcpext2` | `10AC3A41-0000-0000-1E1B-0104B5371F78` | `0x10AC` / `0x413A` | DisplayPort |
- The CoreAudio UID is the EDID vendor followed by the product code byte-swapped (`0x413C` appears as `3C41`).
- At hot-plug (10:56) every DCP's audio link setup failed in the kernel log: `handleStartLink Failed to get workloop` on `DCPEXT0`/`DCPEXT1`, and `Negotiaton already in progress`, `HDCP Startup Failed`, `Phase two failed`, `Failed prepareLink` on `DCPEXT2`. This suggests audio link start is entangled with HDCP negotiation, and that simultaneous hot-plug of three displays collides.
- The DCP nevertheless records `StartLink ... CommandStatus=success` in its `EventLog`, so the failure is silent above the kernel.
- Thereafter every playback attempt fails identically: `coreaudiod` logs `Device <uid> is not running`, `could not establish a timeline after waiting 10000000 microseconds`, `IO Stopped Context ... after 0 frames`, `StartIOThread ... Error: 1937010544` (`'stop'`). The kernel logs `DCPAVAudioDriver::stopIOEngineGated: _hostTime=0` with a `_transferStartTimeStamp` equal to the hot-plug `StartLink` time, i.e. the engine has been dead since plug-in.
- `coreaudiod` itself appears healthy; the evidence points to the DCP firmware / `DCPAVFamily` path. Restarting `coreaudiod` is not expected to help.
- No third-party software is in the audio path: no third-party kexts; HAL plug-ins are Apple's `ParrotAudioPlugin` and Zoom's virtual device; system extensions are Little Snitch (network) and OBS (camera).
- No video decode errors (`AppleAVD`, `VTDecoderXPCService`) were found while video was stalling. Video stalls are believed to be players waiting on the dead audio clock (Firefox's NowPlaying state flapped within milliseconds of each audio failure). Not yet confirmed by switching output and replaying.
- The IORegistry flag `"is running"` on `DCPAVAudioDriver` reads `true` while a client is attempting IO even though no frames move, so it cannot be used alone as a health signal.
- Probe method that works: open an IOProc on the device, play silence, count callbacks. Stuck device: `AudioDeviceStart` blocks ~10s, returns `'stop'`, 0 callbacks. Healthy device (QuadCast S, built-in speakers): ~183 callbacks and ~93k frames in 2s.
- Unified log retention on this machine only reaches back to 2026-11-02 10:27, so pre-27.x behaviour cannot be compared from logs.
- zsh on this machine shadows `log` with a function; use `/usr/bin/log`.
- PoC `relink-mode` (the PoC binary was `dawpoc`, since folded into `fix-display-audio`) (12:50, speaker display 2560x1440@60 -> 800x600@60 for 3s -> back) did NOT repair audio: stuck before and after. The kernel logged no DCP audio link activity at all during the switch (no `StartLink`/`StopLink`, no HDCP), and `_transferStartTimeStamp` stayed at `325510821664`, the original hot-plug value. A mode change is a timing change only and never tears down the audio link, so ladder step 1 is ruled out.
- PoC `relink-enable` (12:51, `SLSConfigureDisplayEnabled` off for 3s, then on, 4s settle) DID repair audio: stuck before, healthy after (370 callbacks, 188,928 frames in 4s), still healthy on a re-probe 20s later. The display returned as main, same ID (4), same mode.
- During that cycle the kernel tore down and re-ran the whole link, including HDCP (`shutdownHDCPBlock`, `abortHDCPNegotiation`, `unprotectLinkGated startupHDCPBlock`). The audio engine then showed a fresh `_transferStartTimeStamp` (`491143642359`) and a non-zero `_hostTime` for the first time since hot-plug.
- The same cycle logs plenty of alarming but apparently harmless lines (`Fixed payload verification failed!`, `preparing video link on dptxwrap failed`, `closeGated failed to get workloop`). Their presence is not a failure signal; judge success by the probe only.
- Conclusion: a software disable/enable of only the affected display, done once the other displays are already up, is enough to re-run audio link setup without collisions. Ladder step 2 is the primary remediation.
- 13:05, second confirmation on a different display: `fix-display-audio` found the U2520D (`dcpext1`, plain DisplayPort, the system-sounds output) still stuck since hot-plug, cycled it with `SLSConfigureDisplayEnabled`, and it probed healthy (277 callbacks, 141,312 frames in 3s). The fix is not specific to the hub's DP-to-HDMI path.
- Probing a stuck device costs ~10s, because `AudioDeviceStart` blocks until `coreaudiod` gives up on the timeline; a healthy probe takes only the probe length.
- 13:07, collateral damage: cycling the U2520D (`dcpext1`) at 13:05 appears to have knocked out the speaker U2518D's audio (`dcpext0`). `dcpext0` had been healthy since its own repair at 12:51, surviving five IO start/stop cycles over 13 minutes, and was healthy at 13:04:50. During the `dcpext1` cycle, `dcpext1` logged `handleStartLink Failed to get workloop`, the same contention seen at hot-plug. From 13:07:16, `dcpext0` showed `_hostTime=0` with an unchanged `_transferStartTimeStamp`, so its engine died without being re-initialised, and `dcpext0` itself logged nothing during the cycle. Single occurrence, so correlation rather than proof, but rebuilding one DCP's link can silently break another DCP's established audio link.
- Mapping an audio device to its screen: each external panel's `AppleCLCD2` (under `dispextN`) has `IOMFBUUID` equal to the CoreAudio UID of that panel's DCP audio device, and `DisplayAttributes.ProductAttributes` holding `LegacyManufacturerID`, `ProductID` and `SerialNumber`, where `SerialNumber` equals `CGDisplaySerialNumber` (the same ten-digit number in both, for the speaker U2518D on CG display 4). `CGDisplayCreateUUIDFromDisplayID` (in ColorSync) returns a different, persistent UUID, so it cannot be used for this match. The EDID-UID's serial field is `0000-0000` on these Dells, so the UID alone cannot separate identical monitors.
- 13:16, second collateral occurrence, in the opposite direction: cycling the speaker U2518D (`dcpext0`) fixed it, but the U2520D (`dcpext1`), healthy at 13:08:21, probed stuck straight after. The other U2518D (`dcpext2`) was also stuck; it was probably never repaired after the 10:56 hot-plug. Two occurrences, one each way, so rebuilding any DCP's link can break another DCP's established audio. This is why auto-fix touches only the watched display.
- 13:28, first run of the menu bar app (auto-fix off): it probed all three display-audio outputs and correctly reported the watched sound output healthy and the other two stuck, an amber state.
- 13:40, Homebrew formula tested from a local tap and tarball (Homebrew 7.0.10, a non-default prefix): `make all` builds inside Homebrew's environment in about 38s, `brew test` passes, and the app bundle's ad-hoc signature survives installation into `libexec` (`codesign --verify --deep --strict`). Launched through `open`, the app runs from the versioned `Cellar` path, not `opt`, which confirms the login item needs `stablePath`. A login item pointing at a missing path was rewritten to the `opt` path on launch.
- Build note: `swiftc` without `-target` stamps the build host's macOS as the minimum (`LC_BUILD_VERSION minos 27.2` here), regardless of `LSMinimumSystemVersion`. Check with `otool -l <binary> | grep -A3 LC_BUILD_VERSION`.
- Each `DCPAVAudioDriver` exposes a `TransferCount` that advanced about once per buffer wrap (12288 frames, about 0.26 s at 48 kHz) on a healthy running engine and stayed still on idle ones. Not yet checked on a stuck engine with a client attempting IO. It is a candidate for detecting the fault without probing.
- The UID's third group is the EDID week and year of manufacture (`041D` = week 4 of 2019, `1E1B` = week 30 of 2017), and the serial group is `0000-0000` on these Dells. Two identical monitors from the same week would therefore share a UID.
- 15:01, launch timing: checking displays one at a time made the first full result take about 24 s with two silent displays (a 3 s delay, then 1 s plus 10 s plus 10 s, all after the welcome was answered). With the list filled instantly, probes run in parallel and no delay, the launch check completes in about 10 s, the cost of one silent probe. Healthy rows update after about 1 s. `loudstation status` went from about 23 s to about 10 s. Concurrent IOProcs on different DCP audio devices showed no interference.
- Private API history, from research on 2026-11-03:
  - **Age:** `CGSConfigureDisplayEnabled` is in CoreGraphics SDK stubs from at least 10.10, and `SLSConfigureDisplayEnabled` in SkyLight from 10.12 (phracker/MacOSX-SDKs). On 27.2, SkyLight exports the SLS name and CoreGraphics re-exports it as the CGS name.
  - **Signature:** every known caller (displayplacer, DisableMonitor, MacTools, LightsOut, blackoutd and others) uses `(CGDisplayConfigRef, CGDirectDisplayID, bool) -> CGError`.
  - **Effective floor:** on Apple Silicon the call only performs a real disconnect from macOS 13 Ventura, which is why BetterDisplay (issue #1524), Lunar (v6.0.0) and blackoutd gate the feature to 13+. A Monterey call would resolve and may return success without tearing the link down, a silent failure, so the macOS 13 minimum is right.
  - **IORegistry:** `DCPAVAudioDriver` with `device UID` already exists on macOS 11.3 (khronokernel/DarwinDumped, MacBookPro17,1). An external `AppleCLCD2` with `IOMFBUUID` and `ProductAttributes` appears in a 2021 M1 dump (Lunar developer's gist).
  - **Hazards reported by other display-utility developers** ([Crisp's display notes](https://github.com/didriksg/Crisp/blob/main/docs/display-notes.md)): an enable can stall WindowServer behind an in-flight DDC read on the DCP, for up to about 30 s; and an enable around clamshell sleep can fail with error 1001 and leave the display off until logout.
  - **Untested:** the audio-relink effect itself below macOS 27.

## 2026-11-05

- 11:47, a probe fooled by an app holding a dead device open. On replug, the speaker U2518D's (`dcpext0`) engine started at 11:47:50.4 while its audio link setup was failing (`handleStartLink Failed to get workloop`), finished one pass through its buffer (0.27 s) and stopped: `_hostTime` never moved again. Spotify kept the device open and running throughout. The check after the replug (11:48:21) and every per-minute check until 11:52 reported it working. Only when Spotify let go (11:52:31) did the next probe's `AudioDeviceStart` block and fail with `'stop'`, nearly five minutes late.
- Why the probe was fooled: when another client already has a device running, `AudioDeviceStart` returns at once and Core Audio keeps calling a new IOProc on an estimated clock, so callbacks arrive and `mSampleTime` advances although no frames reach the hardware.
- `TransferCount` on `DCPAVAudioDriver` is the reliable signal, confirmed on a stuck engine with a client attempting IO (12:17, during a read-only `loudstation status`): both stuck engines read `is running = Yes` within a second of the probe starting, and their `TransferCount` stayed still (1422 and 3) for the whole 10 s blocked start. The healthy, running speaker engine advanced about 4 per second throughout, one per pass through its 12288-frame buffer at 48 kHz.
- The driver also exposes `io buffer frame size` (12288) and `sample rate` as 32.32 fixed point (206158430208000 / 2^32 = 48000), so the expected interval between counter steps (0.256 s) can be computed rather than assumed.
- Timing of an ordinary replug with the earlier settings (2026-11-04): last display event 08:04:46, first silent verdict 08:05:15, confirmation 08:05:36, final probe and cycle 08:05:46, about a minute in all. It was three back-to-back stuck probes of about 10 s each, a 12 s settle and a 10 s wait between the first two probes.
- 13:11, a probe bug that made every check of a working display take about 16 s: the probe waited twice on the same semaphore for `AudioDeviceStart` to return, and the first wait used up the signal, so the second always ran its full 15 s timeout. A 1 s `loudstation status --uid` of the healthy speakers took 16.1 s before the fix and 1.4 s after. A stuck device was unaffected, since its start returns only after the first wait has given up. Seen in the log as a 2 s post-fix probe taking 17 s (2026-11-04 08:05:53 to 08:06:10, and again on 2026-11-05).
- Timing of a clean replug with the fixes above (13:10:41): the first check started 2 s after the plug-in (the sound output moved to the display), the speakers' probe failed after 10.1 s, but the verdict waited 6 s more for a working monitor's probe (the bug). Confirmation took 10 s more, and the screen went off 29 s after the plug-in. The icon stayed blue for 34 s: 3 s off, 4 s settle, 17 s for the speakers' check (the bug), then 10 s re-checking two displays that were already silent before the fix.
